Forward Deployed Engineering Resources

Curated 2026-10-01. Tiered by trust: primary = written or spoken by someone who did the job; secondary = aggregation with a named author and checkable claims; market = job posts and interview reports, useful as evidence of what is actually being hired for, not as analysis. Anything that was marketing dressed as education was left out.

Knowledge — primary (practitioner accounts)

  • Nabeel S. Qureshi, “Reflections on Palantir” (Oct 2024) Eight years as a Palantir FDE, written after leaving. The single best source on what the work actually felt like: the FDE/PD split, “get on a plane first, ask questions later”, learning the customer’s vocabulary (“non-conformities”, “capacity management”), and why Palantir handed out Keith Johnstone’s Impro to teach status-reading. Use for: the discovery lessons, the culture question in a values round, and anything about what separates a good FDE from a competent one.

  • Bob McGrew on the YC Lightcone podcast, “The FDE Playbook for AI Startups” (Sep 2025) McGrew helped invent the role at Palantir, then ran research at OpenAI. The clearest statement of the economic argument: an FDE “bridges the gap between existing product capabilities and the specific needs of the customer”, the Echo/Delta split, and why the model is “doing things that don’t scale, at scale”. Use for: why AI labs hire FDEs now, and the product-generalisation question.

  • Colossus, “The Patriot: Shyam Sankar of Palantir” Long-form history of how Sankar invented the model: the CIA demo loop (fly Sunday, demo Mon–Thu, fly back Friday, code over the weekend), the COIC deployment, Karp’s French-restaurant analogy (“the wait staff are part of the kitchen staff”). Use for: origin story, and the argument that the feedback loop is the product process.

  • PostHog, “WTF is a forward deployed engineer?” Interviews named Palantir FDEs. Carries the cleanest one-line statement of why AI labs need the role — Anjor Kanekar: “There is a gap between foundational model capabilities and the application of those in enterprise use cases where they can add value.” Also a usable FDE-vs-SWE comparison table (60–80% customer time vs 10–20%). Use for: the role-definition lesson and the recruiter screen.

Knowledge — the deployment problem itself

  • MIT Media Lab Project NANDA, “The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025” The 95%-of-pilots-show-no-P&L-impact study. Method: 300+ public initiatives, 52 structured interviews, 153 survey responses, Jan–Jun 2025. Critically, the authors blame brittle workflows, weak contextual learning and poor fit with daily operations — not model capability. Use for: the “why scoping is the job” argument. Cite the method, not just the number; the number is widely overquoted and the sampling is not a random sample of enterprises.

  • Anthropic, “Building effective agents” Already covered in ../learnaiagents. Reach for it here only when an FDE scoping decision turns into an architecture decision — workflow vs agent is a scoping question before it is a design one.

Market evidence (what is actually being hired)

  • Anthropic — Forward Deployed Engineer, Applied AI (job description) The requirements list is the syllabus: ship production applications on Claude; deliver MCP servers, sub-agents and agent skills as customer artifacts; white-glove enterprise deployment; codify repeatable deployment patterns and feed them back to Product; 25–50% travel; 8+ years in technical customer-facing roles. Note the explicit ask for “evaluation frameworks” and “high agency with an ability to navigate ambiguity”. Re-read before every mock interview.

  • Exponent, “Forward Deployed Engineer Interview: The Definitive 2026 Guide” Aggregated loop structure and per-company variations (Anthropic / OpenAI / Palantir). Secondary, commercially motivated, and the rubric language is reconstructed rather than leaked — treat the shape as reliable and the specific wording as approximate. Use for: knowing which rounds exist. The decomposition/open-ended case round is reported as the highest-failure stage; that claim is consistent across the other interview write-ups found, which is weak but non-zero evidence.

Wisdom (communities)

  • MLOps Community Slack — ~20k members. Currently the densest place adjacent FDE practitioners actually talk, because no large FDE-specific community exists yet. Use for: production-deployment war stories, and sanity-checking an architecture before a panel.
  • Palantir alumni — the densest FDE talent network there is, and they answer cold outreach from people who have read their writing. Use for: one 30-minute conversation, after Lesson 02, with a specific question rather than “can I pick your brain”.
  • Not yet joined. Revisit after Lesson 03 — the right moment to join is when you have an artifact to get critiqued, not before.

Gaps

  • No high-trust first-hand account of an AI-lab FDE deployment. Everything primary is Palantir-era. The Anthropic/OpenAI versions of the job are documented only through job posts and interview reports. Watch for Applied AI engineering posts; treat current claims about the AI-lab variant as inference from the JD, and label them as such in lessons.
  • Nothing credible on pricing/scoping economics of an engagement. The one 2026 “playbook” with a runbook and scorecard is paywalled and unverifiable; excluded deliberately.
  • No primary source on the pilot→production security gauntlet (VPC, SSO, data residency, procurement) written by someone who ran one. Needed for Lesson 04 — search vendor security-review documentation and enterprise AI deployment guides before writing it.
